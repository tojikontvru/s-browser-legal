The short version

  1. The app collects no analytics. There are no accounts, no advertising or analytics libraries, no crash reporting.
  2. Bookmarks, history, the download list, settings and the proxy list stay on your device and are never uploaded.
  3. Pages open through a third-party proxy that the app picks automatically. The operator of that proxy sees the sites you visit. The proxies are not ours, we do not vet their owners, and we are not responsible for them.
  4. Nothing leaves the device unless you act. The only message carrying data about you, the report, is assembled on the device and opened in your own Telegram after you press send.
  5. Everything that leaves the device is listed in section 3.

Downloaded files are saved by the Android system download manager into the shared Downloads folder. They stay on the device after the app is uninstalled.

2. Who sees your traffic

The app is built on proxies. That is its purpose and its main risk.

  • Your carrier or provider sees that the device connects to the proxy address. It does not see site names, the proxy receives them.
  • The proxy operator sees the addresses you visit, the time and volume of connections, and your network address. It can read and modify unencrypted pages. It cannot read https pages, but it still learns which domain you opened.
  • Sites see the proxy address instead of yours, plus everything you send them yourself: cookies, logins, form data.

Do not enter passwords, card details or one-time codes while using a free proxy. For important services, use a proxy you trust, or turn the proxy off in settings.

5. Private mode

A private tab: cookies of the sites opened in it are snapshotted before the first request and restored when you leave the mode, the cache is cleared on exit, and the history of that tab is not saved.

What private mode does not do:

  • it does not hide your traffic from the proxy operator;
  • it does not stop the proxy from linking the private tab to your normal tabs, because there is one proxy for the whole app;
  • it does not help against cookies a site already set for you earlier, because those are restored on exit;
  • Android keeps one cookie jar per process, so full isolation is not possible.

In short: private mode removes traces on your device, it does not hide you from the proxy.

6. Ad and tracker blocking

The blocklist ships inside the app, works offline and is never uploaded. The app never asks an outside service whether to block an address.

7. Children

The app is not intended for children under 13. We do not knowingly collect or store data about children.

8. Honest limits

  • The proxies are free and public. Anyone running one can technically read unencrypted traffic and modify responses.
  • A passing check only means the proxy answered three requests and delivered 32 KB. It is not a check of the owner, and not a promise of speed or of no logging.
  • The build is marked debuggable, otherwise Android forbids a browser from routing traffic through a proxy. As a result, on a device with USB debugging enabled a computer can attach to the process. With debugging off, that is not possible. Details in the project README.
  • Pages are rendered by Google's system WebView component. It updates separately from the app and may contact Google servers for safe browsing lists and engine updates. We do not control that and cannot turn it off inside the app.
  • Page translation is performed by Google, not by the app.

9. Your rights

We do not collect personal data, so we hold no records that could be disclosed, corrected or deleted on request. Everything accumulated sits on your device and goes away with the app or through Reset settings.

If you are in a country covered by the GDPR or a similar law, you may contact us and your local supervisory authority. Write to t.me/Safaraligroup and we will answer within 30 days.
